Dog Age Calculator

A 5-year-old medium-size dog is about 36 in human years on the size chart from PetMD (December 13, 2024), and about 57 on the 2020 DNA-methylation formula. This dog age calculator shows both methods side by side. The size chart adjusts for weight, and the formula reflects early development. Neither one is a health check.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Pet Care Numbers

How old is my dog in human years?

Enter your dog’s age and its adult size or weight, and the tool gives you two answers. The size chart gives a human age that depends on how big your dog is. The formula gives one curve for every dog. Use them together to see which life stage your dog is in, not to pin down an exact number.

The size chart

The chart in PetMD’s article by Jennifer Coates, DVM (December 13, 2024) lists whole years from 1 to 16 in four size bands: small (20 lb or less), medium (21–50 lb), large (51–100 lb) and giant (over 100 lb). The calculator uses the chart’s numbers exactly as printed. For ages in between, such as 10 years and 6 months, it reads a straight line between two rows. A 120 lb dog of that age falls in the giant band, halfway between 79 and 86, so the result is 82.5, shown as 83.

The epigenetic formula

The formula comes from Wang et al., Cell Systems (2020): human age = 16 × ln(dog age) + 31. It works from a few weeks old, so it still gives an answer for a 3-month-old puppy (about 9), while the chart starts at 1 year. The same 10½-year-old giant dog comes out at about 69 on the formula.

Why do big dogs age faster than small dogs?

Large and giant dogs reach old age sooner than small dogs, and the size chart shows this from age 6 onward. Nobody knows exactly why. PetMD (2024) says scientists have not yet found the reason. What is known is the pattern, and the chart is built around it.

Up to age 5, the chart puts small, medium and large dogs at the same human age (36 at five years). After that the bands split. At 10 years, a small dog is 56, a medium dog 60, a large dog 66 and a giant dog 79. PetMD gives an example: an 11-year-old Chihuahua is only starting its senior years, while an 11-year-old Great Dane has already outlived its usual life expectancy.

The 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines say the same thing in their own terms: the later life stages depend on breed and size. That is why the calculator asks for your dog’s adult size. For a puppy, the puppy weight calculator shows the average adult weight for its breed.

What is the 16 ln(age) + 31 dog age formula?

The formula is human age = 16 × the natural logarithm of the dog’s age + 31. It was fitted by comparing DNA methylation, chemical marks on DNA that change with age, in dogs and people. Because it is logarithmic, a dog’s first years count for a lot and later years count for less.

Wang and colleagues (Cell Systems, published July 2, 2020) analyzed blood samples from 104 dogs, mostly Labrador retrievers, aged 0.1 to 16 years. The paper reports that about 8 weeks in a dog lines up with about 9 months in a human baby, when milk teeth develop in both. It also found that the Labrador’s expected lifespan of 12 years translates to about 70 human years.

Worked through: a 5-year-old dog is 16 × 1.609 + 31 = 56.75, shown as 57.

Keep two limits in mind. The authors say the study used only Labradors, and that breeds with very different lifespans might need different formulas. The formula also ignores size, so a Chihuahua and a Great Dane of the same age get the same answer.

Is one dog year really seven human years?

No. PetMD (2024) calls the multiply-by-seven rule “simple, but inaccurate” because it counts every year of a dog’s life the same. Dogs mature very quickly at first: PetMD compares a 1-year-old dog to a 15-year-old teenager and a 2-year-old dog to a 24-year-old adult.

The Wang et al. (2020) data also show a curve, not a straight line. The seven-times rule says a 1-year-old dog is 7. The chart says 15 and the formula says 31. At 14 years, the rule gives 98, while the chart gives 72 to 107 depending on size.

After age 2, PetMD puts each dog year at roughly 4 to 8 human years, depending on size. The calculator shows the old rule only for comparison.

When is a dog considered a senior?

AAHA defines a senior dog by lifespan, not by a fixed birthday. The senior stage is the last 25% of estimated lifespan, through end of life, according to the 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines (J Am Anim Hosp Assoc 2019;55:267–290). A giant breed reaches that point years before a small one.

The guidelines list five stages:

  • Puppy: until rapid growth stops, around 6–9 months.
  • Young adult: until physical and social maturity, by 3–4 years in most dogs.
  • Mature adult: until the last 25% of expected lifespan.
  • Senior: the last 25% of expected lifespan.
  • End of life: the terminal stage.

This matches PetMD, which says dogs become seniors at 75% of their life expectancy. AAHA recommends at least twice-yearly exams for senior dogs, and exams every six to twelve months for mature adults. Your veterinarian, who knows your dog’s breed and health, decides when senior care should start. To plan for those visits, try the vet cost calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a dog’s age in human years?

Use a size chart or the Wang et al. formula. For the chart, find your dog’s age and size band: a 5-year-old medium dog is 36. For the formula, multiply the natural log of the age by 16 and add 31: a 5-year-old comes out at about 57. The calculator above does both.

Is 1 year 7 years in dog years?

No. A 1-year-old dog is about 15 in human years on PetMD’s size chart and 31 on the 2020 epigenetic formula. Dogs develop fastest in their first two years, so a single multiplier gets young dogs badly wrong. PetMD calls the seven-year rule simple but inaccurate.

How old is a 14-year-old dog in human years?

It depends on size. On PetMD’s chart, a 14-year-old dog is 72 if small, 78 if medium, 88 if large and 107 if giant. The Wang et al. formula, which ignores size, gives about 73. For most dogs, 14 falls in AAHA’s senior stage, so regular vet checkups matter.

How old is a 9-month-old puppy in dog years?

About 26 in human years on the Wang et al. formula (16 × ln 0.75 + 31). The size chart starts at 1 year, when every dog except the giant breeds is 15, so it has no row for nine months. By AAHA’s definition, a puppy usually stops growing rapidly around 6–9 months.
