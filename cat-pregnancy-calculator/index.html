Cat Pregnancy Calculator

Cats give birth about 65 days after breeding, the figure in the Merck Veterinary Manual’s gestation table. This cat pregnancy calculator adds 65 days to your breeding date to estimate when the kittens are due. Kittens often arrive a few days either side of that date, so ask your veterinarian to confirm the pregnancy.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Pet Care Numbers

How long are cats pregnant?

Pregnancy in cats lasts roughly nine weeks. Merck’s gestation table gives about 65 days, and that is the figure the calculator uses. Merck’s guide for cat owners puts the range at 60 to 65 days. International Cat Care (updated September 26, 2025) gives 63 to 65 days.

For a cat bred on September 16, 2026, the calculator shows about November 20, 2026. Using the 60–65 day range, kittens could come from November 15 onward, so be ready early. A cat bred on January 1, 2028, would be due around March 6, 2028. The calculator counts calendar days and allows for leap years.

Cats are induced ovulators: mating triggers the release of eggs. For that reason, the Merck Veterinary Manual’s cat owner guide (updated August 2025) recommends several matings over 2 to 3 days. If your cat mated on more than one day, enter the first date for the earliest estimate and the last date for the latest.

How can I tell if my cat is pregnant?

Early pregnancy is hard to spot. International Cat Care says there is very little to see in the first few weeks. One of the first signs is nipples that look larger and pinker. After that, a pregnant cat gradually gains weight, and her mammary glands fill as the birth gets closer.

In the last two weeks, most cats start nesting. They look for a quiet, safe and often dark place to give birth. As labor nears, International Cat Care lists these signs:

  • purring and panting
  • restlessness and meowing
  • heavy grooming, especially around the birth canal and teats
  • looking for somewhere quiet, clean and warm

Merck’s owner guide says the first stage of labor lasts 12 to 24 hours. During it, the queen may hide, become restless and build a nest.

International Cat Care advises contacting your vet if:

  • the due date passes without labor
  • there is discolored discharge before the first kitten, or heavy bleeding
  • your cat has strained for 20–30 minutes without delivering a kitten, or cries out in pain

If you are worried at any point during the pregnancy, call your vet straight away.

How can a vet confirm the pregnancy and its stage?

A veterinarian can usually confirm a cat’s pregnancy from about day 21 to 30, either by gently feeling the abdomen or by ultrasound, according to Merck’s owner guide. X-rays taken more than 55 days after breeding help count the kittens.

Merck’s veterinary chapter on pregnancy diagnosis (updated January 2026) explains how ultrasound can estimate the stage. The vet measures the kittens to work out gestational age. For cats, the chapter gives the days left before birth as 61 minus that gestational age. This is useful when you don’t know the breeding date, for example if your cat was outdoors.

Knowing the litter size from X-rays helps you and your vet judge whether every kitten has been born. Ask your vet about feeding, too. International Cat Care recommends switching a pregnant cat to a growth-type diet as soon as you know she is pregnant, and continuing until the kittens are weaned. The cat food calculator covers kittens and adult cats, but your veterinarian should set portions for a pregnant or nursing queen.

Frequently asked questions

How do I work out when my cat’s kittens are due?

Add about 65 days to the breeding date, the approximate gestation length in the Merck Veterinary Manual. A cat bred on September 16, 2026, would be due around November 20, 2026. Merck’s owner guide gives 60 to 65 days, so be prepared a few days early.

How soon can a vet tell if my cat is pregnant?

Merck’s cat owner guide says pregnancy can be detected from about day 21 to 30, by feeling the abdomen or by ultrasound. X-rays are used later, after day 55, to count the kittens.

How many kittens do cats usually have?

International Cat Care says cats usually have about four kittens, but a litter can be anywhere from one to twelve. Kittens may arrive as little as 10 minutes or as much as an hour apart. An X-ray late in pregnancy is the way to know your cat’s count in advance.
