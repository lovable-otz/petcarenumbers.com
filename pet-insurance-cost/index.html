Pet Insurance Cost Calculator

Accident-and-illness pet insurance cost an average of $836 a year per dog and $435 per cat in the U.S. in 2025, according to NAPHIA’s 2026 State of the Industry Report. This calculator compares what you would pay in a year with and without a policy, using your premium, deductible, reimbursement rate and annual limit.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Pet Care Numbers

How much does pet insurance cost?

The U.S. average premium in 2025 was $836 a year (about $70 a month) for a dog and $435 a year (about $36 a month) for a cat on accident-and-illness plans. These figures come from the North American Pet Health Insurance Association’s State of the Industry Report, published June 21, 2026. Accident-only plans cost much less, and plans with wellness cover cost more.

Monthly figures are the yearly averages divided by 12. Your quote will differ. The National Association of Insurance Commissioners says the cost depends on the species, breed, gender, age and location of the pet, and on the coverage and deductible you choose. For the full 2021–2025 trend, see average pet insurance cost. If you have a quote, choose “My quote” in the calculator and enter it.

How do the deductible, reimbursement rate and annual limit work?

Most policies pay you back after you have paid the vet. The calculator follows the standard sequence: covered bills minus the deductible, times the reimbursement rate, capped at the annual limit. What’s left, plus the premium, is what you pay.

Example 1: a dog with a $2,000 year

Premium $836 (the NAPHIA average), $250 annual deductible, 80% reimbursement, no limit, $2,000 in covered bills:

  • $2,000 − $250 = $1,750
  • $1,750 × 80% = $1,400 reimbursed
  • You pay $836 + $2,000 − $1,400 = $1,436, compared with $2,000 uninsured. The policy saves $564 that year.

Example 2: a cat with a $12,000 year and a $5,000 limit

A $45-a-month quote ($540 a year), $500 deductible, 90% reimbursement, $5,000 annual limit, $12,000 in covered bills plus $300 the policy doesn’t cover:

  • ($12,000 − $500) × 90% = $10,350, but the limit caps it at $5,000
  • You pay $540 + $12,300 − $5,000 = $7,840, compared with $12,300 uninsured. The policy saves $4,460, but the limit leaves $7,000 of the covered bills to you.

Read how your own policy applies these terms. The NAIC Pet Insurance Model Act (2022), which states may adopt, asks insurers to disclose deductibles, coinsurance and annual or lifetime limits. The NAIC also notes that some insurers pay from a benefit schedule instead of a percentage of your bill.

When does pet insurance pay for itself?

A policy breaks even when the reimbursement equals the premium. The calculator works this out as deductible + premium ÷ reimbursement rate. Covered bills above that amount mean the policy returned more than it cost that year. Bills below it mean you paid more than you got back.

PolicyBreak-even covered bills
Dog, $836 premium, $250 deductible, 80%$250 + $836 ÷ 0.8 = $1,295
Cat, $540 premium, $500 deductible, 90%$500 + $540 ÷ 0.9 = $1,100
Cat accident-only, $112 premium, $250 deductible, 70%$250 + $112 ÷ 0.7 = $410

A low limit can mean the policy never breaks even. A dog wellness plan at the $1,414 NAPHIA average, 80% reimbursement and a $500 limit can never pay back more than $500. The calculator reports “never within the annual limit” in that case.

In a quiet year, insurance costs you money. The cat accident-only plan above, with a $200 vet bill below the deductible, pays nothing, so you are $112 behind. Insurance is mainly protection against a large bill you can’t predict. NAPHIA’s list of the largest claims its members paid in North America in 2025 is topped by a $66.6K claim for a dog. Is accident-only or accident-and-illness cover better value?

They cover different risks, so compare them by what they pay for. Accident-only plans averaged $190 a year for dogs in 2025, compared with $836 for accident-and-illness (NAPHIA). For cats, the averages were $112 and $435.

NAPHIA describes accident-only cover with examples such as swallowed objects, cuts, car accidents, torn ligaments and poisoning. Accident-and-illness plans add illnesses such as cancer, infections and digestive problems. Plans with embedded wellness may also include vaccinations, early screening tests, nutrition consultations and dental care.

Illnesses make up much of what insurers pay for. NAPHIA’s list of the most common conditions claimed in North America in 2025 starts with digestive (GI) problems for both dogs and cats. For dogs, ear infections and skin conditions come next. For cats, dental disease and urinary tract infections do.

To compare plans for your pet, run the calculator once per plan type with the same vet bills and see how the totals change. Also consider how you would pay a large bill if you had no cover at all.

What does pet insurance usually not cover?

Pre-existing conditions are the most common exclusion. The NAIC (page updated April 16, 2025) says most insurers exclude pre-existing, hereditary and congenital conditions. Many policies also have waiting periods before benefits start.

An NAIC consumer article (February 1, 2018) lists other things plans often leave out:

  • preventive care
  • dental care that isn’t related to an accident or injury
  • treatment of behavioral problems
  • elective procedures

It also notes that some plans have per-illness or lifetime maximums, and that you usually pay the full bill at the vet and are reimbursed later.

The 2022 NAIC model act would ban waiting periods for accidents and cap illness and orthopedic waiting periods at 30 days. It also gives buyers 15 days to review a policy and return it for a refund, provided no claim has been filed. These rules apply only where a state has adopted them, so check your own policy. Enter costs your plan won’t pay in the calculator’s “not covered” box.

Frequently asked questions

How much is typical pet insurance per month?

In 2025, U.S. accident-and-illness plans averaged about $70 a month for a dog ($836 a year) and about $36 for a cat ($435), according to NAPHIA. Accident-only plans averaged about $16 and $9. Your own quote depends on breed, age, location and the options you pick.

Is it worth having pet insurance?

It depends on your bills and your policy. With a $250 deductible and 80% reimbursement, the average $836 dog plan pays for itself once covered bills pass $1,295 in a year. Below that it costs more than it returns. Many owners weigh it as protection against a single large bill.

What are the disadvantages of pet insurance?

You usually pay the vet first and wait to be reimbursed. Most plans exclude pre-existing and hereditary conditions, and many have waiting periods. In a year with few vet bills, you pay more than you get back. The NAIC model act asks insurers to disclose whether premiums rise with a pet’s age or claims.

How much should dog insurance cost?

For comparison, NAPHIA’s 2025 U.S. averages per dog were $190 a year for accident-only, $836 for accident-and-illness and $1,414 with embedded wellness. A quote well above or below these may reflect your dog’s breed, age or location, or a different deductible or limit. Compare the whole policy, not just the price.
