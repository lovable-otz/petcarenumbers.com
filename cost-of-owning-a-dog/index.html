Cost of Owning a Dog or Cat

The cost of owning a dog is about $1,391 a year, or $3,221 in the first year, according to the ASPCA’s 2021 cost table. A cat comes to $1,149 and $1,904. This calculator starts from those figures, line by line. Replace any line with what you actually pay to get your own monthly, yearly and lifetime totals.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Pet Care Numbers

How much does it cost to own a dog per year?

With every ASPCA default, a dog costs $1,391 a year, or $115.92 a month. The biggest lines are pet insurance ($516), food ($300) and routine vet care ($225). These are 2021 prices, and costs have risen since, so treat them as a floor to check against your own bills.

  • Insurance: NAPHIA reports that U.S. accident-and-illness premiums averaged $836 per dog in 2025, well above the ASPCA’s $516. If you don’t insure, enter 0.
  • Food: this depends on your dog’s size and the food you buy. The dog food calculator shows how many cups a day your dog needs, which you can price from the bag.

How much does a dog cost in the first year?

The ASPCA puts a dog’s first year at $3,221. That is the $1,391 yearly total, plus $1,030 in one-time costs, plus $800 for professional grooming ($300) and a dental cleaning ($500).

The one-time costs in the ASPCA table are:

  • spay or neuter: $300
  • initial medical care: $300
  • training: $200
  • collar, leash and harness: $60
  • crate: $60
  • carrier: $50
  • grooming tools: $40
  • microchip: $20

The ASPCA counts grooming and dental care in the first year only. If your dog is groomed professionally or has its teeth cleaned every year, tick “Count dental and grooming every year.” The calculator then adds $800 to every year, and the first year no longer lists them separately.

Here is how much your choices matter. A dog with $600 of food a year, seven days of boarding, and yearly dental and grooming comes to $2,641 a year, $3,671 in the first year and $32,722 over 12 years. With all defaults, 10 years cost $15,740. The number of years is your own estimate.

Is a cat cheaper to own than a dog?

In the ASPCA’s figures, yes. A cat costs $242 less a year ($1,149 against $1,391) and $1,317 less in the first year ($1,904 against $3,221). A cat costs the same or less on every yearly line except litter, which adds $150 a year and has no dog equivalent.

The gap is widest in the first year. The ASPCA lists a cat’s one-time costs at $455, against $1,030 for a dog, because a cat doesn’t need training ($200) or a crate ($60), and spaying or neutering is listed at $150 against $300. A cat’s dental cleaning is $300 against $500, and the ASPCA lists no professional grooming for cats.

Over a lifetime, the number of years changes the picture. With all defaults, a cat kept for 15 years costs $17,990, more than a dog kept for 10 years ($15,740). Insurance follows the same pattern: NAPHIA’s 2025 U.S. average for accident-and-illness cover was $435 for a cat, against $836 for a dog. Choose “Cat” above to see your own totals.

What costs do new owners forget to budget for?

The ASPCA table leaves out several costs. The calculator’s notes say it does not include emergency care, the adoption or purchase price, or pet sitting. It also counts only one day of boarding a year and counts dental and grooming in the first year only.

  • Boarding: the ASPCA total includes one day at $25. Enter the number of days you really expect, and your local daily rate.
  • Dental care: the ASPCA links dental disease to heart and kidney problems and expensive procedures. Budget for cleanings when your vet recommends them.
  • Emergencies: an unplanned illness or injury can cost far more than a routine year. Budget for it through savings or insurance. The pet insurance cost calculator shows how a policy would handle a large bill.
  • Senior care: the 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines recommend at least twice-yearly exams for senior dogs, with blood work every 6 to 12 months, so vet costs tend to rise later in life.

For the vet lines on their own, use the vet cost calculator.

Frequently asked questions

Are dogs expensive to maintain?

A dog costs about $1,391 a year in the ASPCA’s 2021 table, or roughly $116 a month, before emergencies. The first year is higher, at $3,221, because of one-time costs such as spay or neuter, training and a crate. Prices have risen since 2021, so check each line against local prices.

How much does a dog cost per month?

About $116 a month on the ASPCA’s yearly figures ($1,391 ÷ 12), or $96 for a cat. That includes food, routine vet care, parasite prevention, toys, treats, insurance, a license, grooming supplies and one day of boarding. Enter your own prices above for a personal figure.

How much does a dog cost over its lifetime?

It depends on how many years you have your dog. With the ASPCA defaults, 10 years cost $15,740: the $3,221 first year plus nine years at $1,391. With $600 of food, seven boarding days, and yearly dental and grooming, 12 years come to $32,722.

Does the ASPCA estimate include pet insurance?

Yes. The ASPCA’s 2021 table includes $516 a year for a dog and $348 for a cat. NAPHIA’s 2025 U.S. averages for accident-and-illness plans were higher, at $836 and $435. Enter your actual premium, or 0 if you don’t insure, to keep the total realistic.
