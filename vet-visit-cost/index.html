Vet Cost Calculator

Routine vet visits and vaccines cost about $225 a year for a dog and $160 for a cat in the ASPCA’s 2021 cost table, and prices have risen since. This calculator adds up your yearly vet visit cost, including wellness care, parasite prevention and dental cleaning, and you can replace any line with your clinic’s own prices.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Pet Care Numbers

How much does a vet visit cost?

The calculator starts from the ASPCA’s figures. For a dog, wellness visits and vaccines ($225), heartworm, flea and tick prevention ($185) and a dental cleaning ($500) come to $910 a year. For a cat, the same three lines total $600 ($160, $140 and $300).

What does a yearly wellness visit include?

A wellness visit is a full physical exam plus a review of your pet’s care. The 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines list what to cover: lifestyle and safety, behavior, nutrition, parasite control, vaccination, dental health, reproduction, breed-specific conditions and baseline lab work.

Some checks recur. AAHA’s table for adult dogs includes:

  • a heartworm test every year
  • fecal tests for parasites 1 to 4 times a year, depending on lifestyle and preventive use
  • a yearly basic blood panel for mature adults
  • blood work every 6 to 12 months for seniors

The 2021 AAHA nutrition guidelines add a body condition score and muscle condition score at every exam.

For cats, the 2021 AAHA/AAFP guidelines recommend at least one exam a year, and at least one every six months for seniors. They note that a yearly visit is also a chance to discuss microchipping, claw care and disaster preparedness.

How much should I budget for vet bills each month?

Divide your yearly total by 12. With the ASPCA figures and a dental cleaning, a dog comes to $75.83 a month and a cat to $50. In your first year with a dog, adding spay or neuter, first vaccines and a microchip raises the total to $1,530, or $127.50 a month.

Your own numbers may differ widely. For example, a cat in its first year, with $300 for wellness visits, the ASPCA’s first-year items and parasite prevention, a $450 blood test and no dental cleaning, comes to $1,235, about $103 a month.

Plan for the unexpected too, since routine lines don’t cover emergencies. Some owners put that money toward insurance instead. NAPHIA reports U.S. accident-and-illness premiums averaged $836 a year for dogs and $435 for cats in 2025. The pet insurance cost calculator compares a policy with paying your bills yourself. For food, toys and other costs as well, use the cost of owning a dog or cat calculator.

How can I lower my vet bills without skipping care?

Prevention is the main lever. The ASPCA says not to skip the yearly exam, because treating illness costs more, and carries more risk, than preventing it. Its other tips:

  • Tailor vaccines with your vet to your pet’s lifestyle, while keeping those the law requires.
  • Spay or neuter, which the ASPCA says can prevent serious and costly health problems.
  • Look after teeth. The ASPCA links dental disease to heart and kidney problems and expensive procedures.
  • Never use a dog flea or tick product on a cat.
  • Groom at home, with regular nail trims and brushing.
  • Consider insurance while your pet is healthy.

Weight matters too. The AAHA nutrition guidelines say preventing weight gain is easier than treating obesity. The dog food calculator can help you keep portions in check.

Before any planned procedure, ask the clinic for a written estimate, and ask which items are essential and which are optional.

Frequently asked questions

What can I do if I can’t afford a vet visit?

Tell the clinic before treatment starts. Ask for a written estimate, which items are essential now, and whether there are other ways to proceed. In a suspected poisoning, call a poison line first: the ASPCA says a consultation fee may apply, and Pet Poison Helpline charges an $89 incident fee.

What is the average vet fee?

The ASPCA’s 2021 table puts routine medical care, meaning wellness visits and vaccines, at about $225 a year for a dog and $160 for a cat. A dental cleaning is listed at $500 and $300. Prices have risen since 2021 and vary by clinic, so use your clinic’s quotes where you can.

Do I really need to take my dog to the vet every year?

Yes, at least. The 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines recommend exams every six to twelve months for young and mature adult dogs, and at least every six months for seniors. The ASPCA says skipping the yearly exam tends to cost more in the end, because illness is costlier to treat than prevent.

How often should a cat see the vet?

The 2021 AAHA/AAFP Feline Life Stage Guidelines recommend at least one exam a year for every cat, and at least one every six months once a cat is over 10. Cats with chronic conditions may need to go more often.
