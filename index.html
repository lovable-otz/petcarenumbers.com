Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this site names its publisher and date. The calorie formulas, gestation windows and chocolate thresholds come from the Merck Veterinary Manual. Dog ages come from PetMD’s size chart (December 13, 2024) and the Wang et al. formula (Cell Systems, 2020). Cat ages come from International Cat Care (updated November 28, 2025), and life stages from the AAHA and AAHA/AAFP guidelines. Breed weights come from Andersson et al. (Scientific Reports, 2023). Insurance premiums are NAPHIA’s 2025 U.S. averages, and ownership costs are the ASPCA’s 2021 table. The NAPHIA figures are refreshed each June, when the new report comes out. Merck, ASPCA and the other sources are rechecked at least once a year. This site is not a veterinary practice and is maintained by Pet Care Numbers. Every formula is on the methodology page.

Common questions

What does pet insurance not pay for?

Most insurers exclude pre-existing, hereditary and congenital conditions, the NAIC says. More in Average Pet Insurance Cost.

Does pet insurance cover spaying?

Usually not under a standard accident-and-illness plan: the NAIC says plans often exclude elective procedures and preventive care. More in Average Pet Insurance Cost.

What is a good monthly price for pet insurance?

NAPHIA’s 2025 averages give a benchmark: about $70 a month for a dog and $36 for a cat on accident-and-illness plans. A lower price may mean a higher deductible, a lower reimbursement rate or a tighter limit. More in Average Pet Insurance Cost.

Is a $5,000 annual limit enough?

It covers many years, but not a very expensive one. In the calculator’s example, a $12,000 year with a $500 deductible and 90% reimbursement would pay $10,350 without a cap. A $5,000 limit cuts that to $5,000. More in Average Pet Insurance Cost.

Is pet insurance really worth the cost?

It depends on your pet’s bills and how you would cover a large one. The average $836 dog plan, with a $250 deductible and 80% reimbursement, breaks even at $1,295 in covered bills in a year. More in Average Pet Insurance Cost.

How do I calculate a cat’s age in human years?

Count the first two years as 24 human years, then add 4 for each year after. A 6-year-old cat is 24 + 4 × 4 = 40. For kittens, use International Cat Care’s month-by-month table: 3 months is 4 and 6 months is 10. More in Cat Age Calculator.
