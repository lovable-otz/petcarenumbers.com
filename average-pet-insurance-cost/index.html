Average Pet Insurance Cost

The typical pet insurance cost in the U.S. in 2025 was $836 a year per dog and $435 per cat for accident-and-illness plans, according to NAPHIA’s State of the Industry Report (June 21, 2026). That works out to about $70 and $36 a month. The table above lists all three plan types from 2021 to 2025.

How much does pet insurance cost per month on average?

Divide NAPHIA’s yearly averages by 12. In 2025, accident-and-illness cover averaged $69.67 a month for a dog and $36.25 for a cat. Accident-only cover averaged $15.83 and $9.33. Plans with embedded wellness averaged $117.83 and $71.58.

These are averages per insured pet. NAPHIA says its data covers about 99% of pet health insurance premium written in the U.S. and Canada, compiled by Willis Towers Watson from member surveys and public sources.

An average is useful for checking whether a quote is in the usual range, but it can’t tell you what your pet will cost to insure. For that, get quotes and compare them in the pet insurance cost calculator, which also shows what a policy would pay back on your vet bills.

Why does insurance cost more for dogs than cats?

In 2025, the average accident-and-illness premium for a dog ($836) was about 1.9 times the average for a cat ($435). NAPHIA reports the gap but doesn’t explain it. The NAIC lists species as one of the factors behind a premium, along with breed, gender, age, location and the coverage chosen.

Other published figures point the same way. The ASPCA’s 2021 cost table puts routine medical care at $225 a year for a dog and $160 for a cat, and a dental cleaning at $500 and $300. Spaying or neutering costs $300 for a dog and $150 for a cat.

Dogs also dominate the market. NAPHIA says dogs made up 74.9% of insured U.S. pets and 84.5% of premium volume in 2025. Cats are the faster-growing group: the number of insured cats rose 16.3% in 2025, compared with 6.8% for dogs.

How fast have premiums risen since 2021?

The average U.S. accident-and-illness premium for a dog rose from $625 in 2021 to $836 in 2025, up 33.8%. It rose 11.0% in 2024 and 11.5% in 2025. For cats, the average went from $370 to $435 (up 17.6%), with a 12.6% jump in 2025.

These are averages across all policies in force, so they move with the mix of pets and plans as well as with prices. U.S. premium volume reached about $5.7 billion at the end of 2025, up 19.7%, while the number of insured pets grew 9.0%. NAPHIA says premium growth keeps outpacing pet growth because the average premium rises each year.

Why might your quote differ from the average?

Your quote is priced for your pet and your choices. The average is not. The NAIC names the pet’s species, breed, gender, age and location, plus the coverage and deductible you pick, as factors in the cost.

Things that can move a quote away from the average:

  • Deductible, reimbursement rate and limit. A lower deductible or a higher reimbursement rate means the insurer pays more, which usually costs more.
  • Plan type. Adding wellness cover raised the 2025 dog average from $836 to $1,414.
  • Age and claims. The NAIC Pet Insurance Model Act (2022) asks insurers to disclose whether they raise premiums or reduce cover based on claim history, the pet’s age or a move to a new area.
  • Where you live. NAPHIA’s 2025 figures show insured pets are concentrated in a few states: California alone accounted for 18.1% of U.S. insured pets.

Frequently asked questions

What does pet insurance not pay for?

Most insurers exclude pre-existing, hereditary and congenital conditions, the NAIC says. Its consumer guide adds that many plans leave out preventive care, dental care not caused by an accident, behavioral treatment and elective procedures. Costs during a waiting period and above a plan’s limit are not paid either.

Does pet insurance cover spaying?

Usually not under a standard accident-and-illness plan: the NAIC says plans often exclude elective procedures and preventive care. Plans with wellness cover pay for some routine care, and NAPHIA lists vaccinations, screening tests and dental care as examples. Check the plan’s own list. The ASPCA’s 2021 table puts spay/neuter at $300 for a dog and $150 for a cat.

What is a good monthly price for pet insurance?

NAPHIA’s 2025 averages give a benchmark: about $70 a month for a dog and $36 for a cat on accident-and-illness plans. A lower price may mean a higher deductible, a lower reimbursement rate or a tighter limit. Compare what each plan pays on the same bills, not just the premium.

Is a $5,000 annual limit enough?

It covers many years, but not a very expensive one. In the calculator’s example, a $12,000 year with a $500 deductible and 90% reimbursement would pay $10,350 without a cap. A $5,000 limit cuts that to $5,000. NAPHIA’s 2025 North American lists of the ten largest claims ran from $42.3K to $66.6K for dogs and from $24.5K to $51.6K for cats.

Is pet insurance really worth the cost?

It depends on your pet’s bills and how you would cover a large one. The average $836 dog plan, with a $250 deductible and 80% reimbursement, breaks even at $1,295 in covered bills in a year. The pet insurance cost calculator runs this test with your own numbers.
