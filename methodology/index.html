Methodology

This page sets out each formula exactly as the calculators apply it, and the source and date behind every outside figure. All calculations run in your browser. Weights are converted at 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg and 1 oz = 28.349523125 g. Dates are counted in calendar days.

Dog age calculator

Size chart: the human-age values for dogs aged 1 to 16 years come from the chart in PetMD’s article by Jennifer Coates, DVM (published December 13, 2024). The chart is an image. It was transcribed cell by cell, and the transcription is kept with the site’s source files. The size bands are small (20 lb or less), medium (21–50 lb), large (51–100 lb) and giant (over 100 lb). If you enter an adult weight, it sets the band: 20 lb or less is small, up to 50 lb is medium, up to 100 lb is large, and anything heavier is giant. Ages between two whole years are read on a straight line between the two rows. Ages under 1 year and over 16 years have no chart value.

Formula: human age = 16 × ln(dog age in years) + 31, from Wang et al. (Cell Systems 2020;11(2):176–185.e6). The study used 104 dogs, mostly Labrador retrievers, aged 0.1 to 16 years, so the formula is defined from 0.1 years. Above 16 years, the tool warns that it is going beyond the study’s data. It does not adjust for size.

Comparison: the old rule, age × 7, is shown for reference only. Senior status follows the 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines: the last 25% of the dog’s estimated lifespan. That definition has no fixed age, so the tool gives no senior cut-off.

Cat age calculator

Human age comes from the table in International Cat Care’s “How to tell your cat’s age in human years” (updated November 28, 2025). The table runs month by month to 7 months, then continues through 25 years. A newborn is read as 0. Ages between two rows are read on a straight line. After 25 years (116), the tool adds 4 human years per year. The charity’s stage name is taken from the last row reached. The AAHA/AAFP stage (2021 Feline Life Stage Guidelines) is: kitten under 1 year, young adult from 1 year up to but not including 7, mature adult from 7 through 10, and senior over 10.

Dog and cat food calculators

RER = 70 × (weight in kg)^0.75 (Merck Veterinary Manual, Nutritional Requirements of Small Animals, updated September 2024). Daily calories = RER × factor. The factors come from Merck’s table of daily maintenance energy requirements:

  • Dogs: intact 1.8, neutered 1.6, obesity prone 1.4, puppy under 4 months 3, puppy over 4 months 2.
  • Cats: intact 1.4, neutered 1.2, obesity prone 1.0, kitten 2.5.

Two dog options use Table 1 of the Ohio State University Veterinary Medical Center’s Basic Calorie Calculator, which is an image transcribed for this site. Weight loss uses 1.0 × the RER of the ideal weight you enter. For active or working dogs, you choose a factor from 2.0 to 5.0, and values outside that range are clamped. With “treats” ticked, 10% of calories are set aside, following the 2021 AAHA Nutrition and Weight Management Guidelines, and the food portion is worked out from the remaining 90%. Portion = food calories ÷ kcal per cup or per can, or ÷ kcal per kg × 1,000 for grams. Per meal = portion ÷ meals, with meals rounded to a whole number of at least 1. Merck says a pet may need up to 30% more or fewer calories, and OSU says up to 50%.

Puppy weight calculator

The tool shows the least-squares mean young-adult weight for the chosen breed and sex from Table 1 of Andersson et al., Scientific Reports 2023;13:443. The data come from Sweden’s hip-screening programme, 2007–2016: 114,568 dogs of 72 breeds, weighed at 12–24 months, or 18–30 months for giant breeds. Pounds = kg ÷ 0.45359237. Share = today’s weight ÷ breed average × 100. The published table prints two impossible female values, 1.1 kg for the Alaskan Malamute and 233.5 kg for the American Staffordshire Terrier. Both are withheld, and the tool shows no figure for them.

Dog and cat pregnancy calculators

Due window = the date entered plus a range of days from the Merck Veterinary Manual:

  • breeding date: 58–72 days
  • ovulation: 62–64 days
  • LH surge: 64–66 days
  • first day of diestrus: 56–58 days
  • cat breeding date: 65 days

The dog breeding and ovulation windows and the cat figure come from Merck’s Approximate Gestation Periods table. The LH surge and diestrus windows come from Breeding Management of Bitches (updated July 2025).

Dog chocolate toxicity calculator

mg eaten = grams × mg of methylxanthines (theobromine + caffeine) per gram, from the Merck Veterinary Manual’s Chocolate Toxicosis in Animals (full review September 2024, updated February 2026):

  • cocoa powder 28.5
  • unsweetened chocolate 15.5
  • cocoa bean hulls 9
  • semisweet or dark 5.3–5.6
  • milk 2.3
  • white 0.04

For a bar labeled with a cocoa percentage, mg per gram = 15.5 × cocoa %, which is the manual’s own method. Dose = mg ÷ body weight in kg. The higher end of the range is compared with Merck’s levels: 20 mg/kg for mild signs, 40–50 mg/kg for heart effects and 60 mg/kg or more for seizures. The first line of every result tells you to call your veterinarian or a poison line, and no result is described as safe. The phone numbers were checked on the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center and Pet Poison Helpline websites on September 16, 2026.

Pet insurance cost calculator

Reimbursed = (covered bills − annual deductible, not below 0) × reimbursement rate, capped at the annual limit if one is entered. Cost with insurance = premium + covered bills + uncovered bills − reimbursed. Cost without insurance = covered bills + uncovered bills. Break-even covered bills = deductible + premium ÷ reimbursement rate. If breaking even would need a reimbursement (equal to the premium) larger than the annual limit, the tool reports that the policy never breaks even within the limit. The default premium is NAPHIA’s 2025 U.S. average annual premium per pet for the chosen plan and species. A monthly quote is multiplied by 12. Per-condition deductibles, waiting periods and exclusions are not modeled.

Vet cost and cost-of-ownership calculators

Each blank line uses the ASPCA’s “Cutting Pet Care Costs” table (2021 update). Your own figures replace them line by line. Vet cost = wellness visits and vaccines + parasite prevention, plus a dental cleaning if ticked. First-year pets also add spay or neuter, first vaccines and exams, and a microchip. Any extra costs you list are added last.

Ownership: yearly total = the ASPCA yearly lines (litter for cats only) + boarding days × daily rate. First year = yearly total + one-time costs + dental cleaning + professional grooming (dogs). If you tick “every year,” dental and grooming move into the yearly total instead. Lifetime = first year + yearly total × (years − 1). With every default, the tool reproduces the ASPCA totals: $1,391 a year and $3,221 in the first year for a dog, and $1,149 and $1,904 for a cat. No inflation adjustment is applied, because the ASPCA publishes none.

Data and refresh schedule

  • NAPHIA State of the Industry Report 2026 (published June 21, 2026; 2025 U.S. data). Refreshed each June, when the new report is published.
  • Merck Veterinary Manual topics: chocolate (updated February 2026), nutrition (September 2024), breeding management (July 2025), pregnancy determination (January 2026) and dystocia (July 2026). Review dates are checked at least once a year, and figures are updated if a topic changes.
  • ASPCA cost table (2021 update), International Cat Care (November 28, 2025) and PetMD (December 13, 2024). Checked yearly for a newer edition.
  • AAHA and AAHA/AAFP guidelines (2019 canine life stage, 2021 feline life stage, 2021 nutrition), Wang et al. (2020) and Andersson et al. (2023). Updated when a new edition or correction is published.
  • Poison-line phone numbers and fees. Rechecked at every update.
