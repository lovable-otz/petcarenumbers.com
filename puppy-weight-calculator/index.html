Puppy Weight Calculator

Male Labrador Retrievers averaged 31.1 kg (68.6 lb) as young adults in a 2023 Scientific Reports study of 114,568 dogs in 72 breeds. This puppy weight calculator shows that breed-and-sex average, and how far along your puppy is today as a share of it. It gives an average for the breed, not a forecast for your own puppy.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Pet Care Numbers

How big will my puppy get?

Pick your puppy’s breed and sex, and the tool shows the average weight of young adult dogs of that breed and sex. Enter your puppy’s weight today to see what share of that average it has reached. A 34.3 lb male Labrador puppy, for example, is at 50% of the 68.6 lb breed average.

The figures come from Table 1 of Andersson and colleagues (Scientific Reports, January 9, 2023). The authors used body weights recorded in Sweden’s official hip dysplasia screening program from 2007 to 2016. Most dogs were weighed at 12 to 24 months. Giant breeds were weighed at 18 to 30 months.

The study found a significant weight difference between the sexes in 54 of the 72 breeds (75%), with males heavier. That is why the calculator asks for sex.

The published table prints two female weights that can’t be right: 1.1 kg for the Alaskan Malamute and 233.5 kg for the American Staffordshire Terrier. The calculator does not show a figure for either.

At what age do puppies stop growing?

It depends on size. A 2017 study of growth charts in PLOS One (Salt et al.) notes that smaller dogs reach maturity at 8 to 12 months, while larger breeds can take up to 24 months to reach adult body weight. Andersson and colleagues also note that dogs of different breeds reach adult weight at different ages.

Veterinary guidance follows the same pattern. The 2019 AAHA Canine Life Stage Guidelines end the puppy stage when rapid growth stops, at about 6 to 9 months depending on breed and size. The 2021 AAHA nutrition guidelines say small and medium dogs usually reach skeletal maturity around 1 year, and some large and giant breeds not until closer to 15–16 months.

Growth doesn’t happen at a steady pace, so a puppy’s share of adult weight doesn’t rise evenly with age. A large-breed puppy at 50% may have much further to go, in months, than a small-breed puppy at 50%.

Why can't a calculator predict my puppy's exact adult weight?

A breed average describes a whole population, not one dog. An average sits in the middle of a spread, so many individual dogs of the same breed and sex end up above or below it. The study behind this tool also has limits that affect how you should read it.

  • Which breeds it covers. The authors say the breeds were mostly medium to large, because of how the screening program chose them. There are no toy or small breeds, so there is no figure for a Chihuahua or a Yorkshire Terrier.
  • Body condition. The dogs’ body condition was not recorded, so the authors say it is unknown whether the averages reflect ideal weight.
  • Changes over time. Weight changed significantly over the 10 years in 33 breeds (45%).

For a mixed-breed puppy, no breed row fits well. Your veterinarian can estimate adult size from the puppy’s growth over several visits.

How can I tell if my puppy is growing at a healthy rate?

Weigh your puppy regularly and ask your veterinarian to plot the weights over time. The growth charts published by Salt and colleagues (2017) were built so veterinary teams can monitor growth objectively and spot puppies with possible growth problems. They were built from Banfield Pet Hospital records and cover 12 weeks to 2 years of age.

Weight alone doesn’t show whether a puppy is lean. The AAHA 2021 guidelines recommend a body condition score at every exam. For giant-breed puppies, they advise keeping a lean body condition and avoiding too much calcium.

Feeding drives growth, so check portions as your puppy grows. The dog food calculator uses 3 × RER under 4 months and 2 × RER after that. Your vet should confirm the amounts. Once your dog is grown, the dog age calculator uses its adult weight to pick the right size band.

Frequently asked questions

How can I estimate my puppy’s adult weight?

Use the breed average as a guide. Pick your puppy’s breed and sex above to see the young-adult average from the 2023 Swedish study, then enter today’s weight to see what share of it your puppy has reached. The calculator can’t give your puppy’s exact adult weight, and it has no figures for toy or small breeds.

At what age can you tell how big a puppy will be?

The picture gets clearer as growth slows. Small dogs reach maturity at about 8 to 12 months, and larger breeds can take up to 24 months to reach adult weight, according to Salt et al. (2017). Your vet can track your puppy’s own growth over several visits.

How much should a healthy puppy weigh?

No single weight fits every puppy, because it depends on breed, sex and age. A healthy puppy grows steadily and keeps a lean body condition. AAHA recommends a body condition score at every exam, which tells your vet more than weight alone.

At what age do puppies reach 50% of their adult weight?

The sources used here don’t give one age for every breed. Growth timing varies with size: small dogs finish growing much earlier than large and giant breeds. The calculator shows your puppy’s current share of the breed average, but that share doesn’t translate into a fixed age.
