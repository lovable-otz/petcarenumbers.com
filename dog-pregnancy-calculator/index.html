Dog Pregnancy Calculator

Dogs give birth 58 to 72 days after breeding, or 62 to 64 days after ovulation, according to the Merck Veterinary Manual. This dog pregnancy calculator turns a breeding, ovulation, LH surge or diestrus date into a due-date window. Your veterinarian confirms the pregnancy and plans the birth with you.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Pet Care Numbers

How long are dogs pregnant?

Pregnancy in dogs lasts about nine weeks. How precisely you can predict the birth depends on which date you count from. Merck gives 62–64 days from ovulation. Counted from a breeding date, when the stage of the heat cycle isn’t known, the range widens to 58–72 days.

The calculator uses four windows from two Merck sources, the gestation table and Breeding Management of Bitches (Autumn P. Davidson, DVM; updated July 2025):

Days are counted on the calendar, including across a year end. Treat the window as the time to be ready, not a promised delivery day. For a cat, use the cat pregnancy calculator.

Why is the due date a window rather than a single day?

A breeding date is not the day of conception. Merck explains that fresh dog semen can live in the vagina for 9 days or more. That means a breeding even before the fertile window can still lead to pregnancy. So Merck says breeding dates do not line up with whelping dates as closely as ovulation does.

The eggs also take time. According to Merck, dogs ovulate 2–3 days after the LH surge, the eggs take another 2–3 days to mature, and a mature egg lives for 2–3 days. Fertilization can happen anywhere within that span, which is why a breeding-date estimate covers 15 days.

If you only know the breeding date, or there were several breedings, the calculator shows the full 58–72 day window. Enter the first breeding date for the earliest possible start. Your vet can narrow the window later.

How does ovulation timing make the due date more accurate?

Hormone tests pin down the start of the pregnancy. A due date counted from the LH surge (64–66 days) or from ovulation (62–64 days) is only three days wide, compared with 15 days from a breeding date. Your veterinarian runs these tests during the heat cycle.

The Merck chapter describes three tools:

  • Progesterone blood tests, taken every 2 days, can catch the first rise in progesterone (usually 1.5–2.5 ng/mL), which lines up with the LH surge.
  • Daily LH tests find the exact day of the surge and are an accurate way to time breeding.
  • Vaginal cytology, a check of vaginal cells, is repeated every 2–3 days. Tracking it to the first day of diestrus lets the vet calculate the due date (56–58 days later) accurately.

How can a vet confirm a dog is pregnant?

Your veterinarian has several ways to confirm a pregnancy, each at its own stage. Merck says ultrasound works best at 25–35 days. Before day 21 it can miss a pregnancy. It can also show the puppies’ heartbeats and estimate how far along the pregnancy is.

From Merck’s Pregnancy Determination in Bitches and Queens (updated January 2026):

  • Feeling the belly: in a cooperative dog, the swellings can be felt at around day 21. They become less distinct after days 35–38.
  • Relaxin blood test: detects placentas after 22–27 days.
  • Ultrasound: best at 25–35 days. Measurements of the puppies let the vet estimate gestational age and the days left before birth.
  • X-rays: puppies’ skeletons are not visible until about day 42–45. X-rays taken after day 55 are the best way to count the litter.

Knowing the litter size helps you and your vet know when whelping is finished. After the birth, the puppy weight calculator shows average adult weights for 72 breeds.

When should I call the vet during whelping?

Call your veterinarian whenever labor doesn’t look right, and agree on a plan and an emergency number before the due date. Merck’s chapter on difficult births (updated July 2026) lists warning signs, including:

  • Pregnancy longer than 70 days from a known breeding date.
  • No puppies 24–36 hours after the dog’s body temperature drops.
  • More than 30 minutes between puppies while clear contractions continue.
  • More than 2 hours between puppies with no contractions.
  • Four hours of strong or weak labor with no puppy born.
  • Whelping that lasts more than 24 hours in total.
  • Heavy bleeding during whelping, or dark green or foul-smelling discharge before the first puppy.

If your dog is in any of these situations, call your vet or an emergency clinic right away rather than waiting. Your vet will also tell you what to watch for in your own dog, especially if a C-section is planned.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate my dog’s due date?

Add 58 to 72 days to the breeding date, or 62 to 64 days to the ovulation date, as the Merck Veterinary Manual gives. A dog bred on September 16, 2026, would be due between November 13 and November 27, 2026. Enter your date above to get your window.

How can I tell how many weeks my dog is pregnant?

Count from the breeding date for a rough idea. For a firmer answer, ask your vet for an ultrasound: Merck says fetal measurements let the vet estimate gestational age and predict the days left before birth. Ultrasound is most useful at 25–35 days.

Can I use a human pregnancy test on my dog?

The veterinary sources used here don’t describe using a human test on dogs. The blood test they do describe is a relaxin test, which Merck says detects placentas after 22–27 days of pregnancy. Ask your veterinarian for that test or for an ultrasound.

Can I predict how big my puppies will get?

For purebred puppies, you can see the breed average. The puppy weight calculator shows the average young-adult weight by breed and sex from a 2023 study of 114,568 dogs in 72 breeds. It is an average, not a forecast, and has no data for toy or small breeds.
