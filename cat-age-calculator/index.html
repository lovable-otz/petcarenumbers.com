Cat Age Calculator

An 8-year-old cat is about 48 in human years, according to International Cat Care (updated November 28, 2025). The first two years count as 24, and each year after that adds 4. This cat age calculator converts any age and shows your cat’s life stage under the 2021 AAHA/AAFP feline guidelines.

Updated September 20, 2026 · By Pet Care Numbers

How old is my cat in human years?

Take 24 human years for the first two years, then add 4 for every year after. A 5-year-old cat is 36, a 10-year-old is 56 and a 15-year-old is 76. The calculator uses the month-by-month table from International Cat Care, so it handles kittens and in-between ages too.

The charity’s table goes up to 25 years, which it counts as 116. For older cats the calculator keeps adding 4 a year, so a 27-year-old cat is 124. For ages that fall between two rows, the tool reads a straight line between them. A cat of 1 year and 3 months falls between 15 (at 12 months) and 21 (at 18 months), so it comes out at 18.

Why are the first two years worth 24 human years?

Cats do most of their growing up in their first two years. International Cat Care describes kittens as growing quickly and learning about the world, and compares its junior stage (7 months to 2 years) to human teens and young adults. That is why the chart counts 24 years by age two and only 4 a year after that.

The chart moves fastest at the start. A 1-month-old kitten is 1, a 3-month-old is 4 and a 6-month-old is 10. By the first birthday, a cat is 15. The second year adds 9 more, and from then on each year adds 4.

This is also why multiplying by seven gets cats wrong. It would make a 1-year-old cat 7 and a 2-year-old 14, well behind the physical maturity these ages actually bring. International Cat Care’s table does not use a multiplier at all.

What are the life stages of a cat?

The 2021 AAHA/AAFP Feline Life Stage Guidelines name four stages based on age: kitten (birth up to 1 year), young adult (1 through 6 years), mature adult (7 to 10 years) and senior (over 10 years). A fifth stage, end of life, can happen at any age.

International Cat Care uses six names with different cut-offs, and the calculator shows both:

  • Kitten: 0–6 months
  • Junior: 7 months to 2 years
  • Adult: 3–6 years
  • Mature: 7–10 years, which the charity compares to people in their mid-40s to mid-50s
  • Senior: 11–14 years
  • Super senior: 15 years and over

The two systems mostly line up. They differ for cats just past 10: a cat of 10 years and 6 months is a senior under AAHA/AAFP (over 10) but still mature on the International Cat Care chart. The AAHA/AAFP task force stresses that any age groupings are arbitrary lines along a spectrum. Some cats over 10 stay in excellent condition and can be treated as mature adults at the veterinarian’s discretion.

When is a cat a senior, and how often should it see the vet?

Under the AAHA/AAFP guidelines, a cat becomes a senior once it is over 10 years old. The task force recommends at least one exam a year for every cat, and at least one every six months for seniors. Cats with chronic conditions need visits more often.

The guidelines say the life stages help vets focus on the changes each age brings. For young adults, that means preventing obesity. For mature and senior cats, it means watching more closely for early kidney disease (AAHA/AAFP, published February 17, 2021).

The calculator gives the life stage as a starting point for that conversation. Your veterinarian decides how often your cat should be seen. Senior visits cost money, so you may want to plan for them with the vet cost calculator.

Frequently asked questions

How do I calculate a cat’s age in human years?

Count the first two years as 24 human years, then add 4 for each year after. A 6-year-old cat is 24 + 4 × 4 = 40. For kittens, use International Cat Care’s month-by-month table: 3 months is 4 and 6 months is 10.

Is 1 year 7 years in cat years?

No. International Cat Care’s chart puts a 1-year-old cat at 15 human years and a 2-year-old at 24. Cats mature much faster than a flat multiplier suggests in their first two years, then slow to about 4 human years for each calendar year.

Is 12 years old considered old for a cat?

Yes. A 12-year-old cat is 64 in human years, and the 2021 AAHA/AAFP guidelines count any cat over 10 as a senior. International Cat Care’s chart also calls 11 to 14 the senior years. The guidelines recommend a vet visit at least every six months at this stage.

How old is a 2-year-old cat in human years?

About 24. That is the end of International Cat Care’s junior stage, and a young adult under AAHA/AAFP. From here, each extra year adds about 4 human years, so the cat will be 28 at three and 32 at four.
